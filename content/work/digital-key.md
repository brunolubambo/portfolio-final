## Overview

The problem: the process of purchasing a "digital key" (NFT) to access an autonomous urban mobility service was overly complex and inaccessible for users without Web3 knowledge. Blockchain terminology, wallet creation, and cryptocurrency transactions acted as a significant barrier to entry, hindering customer conversion and limiting growth.

**Tools used:** Figma · Notion · Miro · Google Suite

---

## The Problem

### Affected users

- People who want to use the urban mobility club but don't understand Web3 or crypto
- Executives and frequent travellers seeking convenient transport, deterred by technical jargon
- Owners of digital keys who want to resell or rent them, but face a complicated process

**Business impact:** Low customer acquisition, limited market reach, and a perception of the product as overly niche.

---

## Research & Insights

### Target Audience

The primary audience is executives and frequent travellers — typically men aged 18–44, high income. A critical insight: **only 16% of this group has ever bought cryptocurrency**. This immediately established the principle of avoiding Web3 jargon and designing as if blockchain doesn't exist from the user's perspective.

<img src="/images/work/digital-key/csd-matrix.png" alt="CSD Matrix — Certainties, Suppositions and Doubts for the Digital Key project" class="invert" />

### CSD Matrix

**Certainties:**
- Users find the current NFT purchase process complex
- Absence of traditional payment options is a barrier
- Simplifying the process will increase adoption
- Most users don't understand blockchain or crypto

**Suppositions:**
- A white-label solution embedded in the app will feel native
- Users will trust a simplified process if security signals are present
- Renaming "NFT" to "VIP Key" will significantly increase acceptance
- Short explanatory videos will reduce confusion at key steps

**Doubts:**
- How will users react to a "digital key" vs. a traditional subscription model?
- What are the specific abandonment points in the current flow?
- What is the optimal placement and length for explanatory content?

## Design Process: Double Diamond

<img src="/images/work/digital-key/double-diamond.png" alt="Double Diamond design process — Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver" class="invert" />

**Discover**
Mapped barriers and friction points in the existing user journey. Identified "payment difficulty" and "complexity in the digital key acquisition process" as the two primary pain points.

**Define**
Identified the core design challenge: *Simplify the digital key purchase process so that any user — regardless of technical knowledge — can complete it using familiar payment methods.*

**Develop**
Brainstormed solutions focused on: simplified purchase via credit/debit/Pix, automatic wallet creation, and complete abstraction of blockchain steps. Began prototyping and validating initial concepts.

**Deliver**
Implemented an optimised experience where the user acquires the digital key simply, without needing to understand Web3. Wallet creation is handled automatically through a white-label integration (e.g., MoonPay). The user never sees blockchain — they see a purchase flow.

## Key Design Decisions

### Renaming "NFT" → "VIP Key"
The terminology shift was one of the highest-leverage decisions in the project. "NFT" carries connotations of complexity, speculation, and exclusivity in the wrong sense. "VIP Key" is intuitive, aspirational, and self-explanatory in the context of an urban mobility service.

### Abstracting the Wallet
Instead of requiring users to create and manage a crypto wallet — a multi-step process with high abandonment — we automated wallet creation entirely behind the scenes. The user completes a standard payment; the system handles the rest.

### Traditional Payment First
Pix and credit/debit card were positioned as the primary and default payment methods. Crypto payment was available but deprioritised visually. This reversed the original product's implicit hierarchy.

### Security Signals Over Technical Explanation
Rather than explaining how blockchain works, the design focused on communicating *that* the transaction is secure — through familiar trust indicators (SSL badges, transaction receipts, verified emblems) rather than technical documentation.

## Value Proposition

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Buy an NFT with a crypto wallet | Buy a VIP Key with your card or Pix |
| Requires blockchain knowledge | Zero technical knowledge required |
| Manual wallet creation | Automatic wallet, invisible to the user |
| Complex multi-step flow | Streamlined purchase flow |
| Targeted at Web3 users | Accessible to any user |
